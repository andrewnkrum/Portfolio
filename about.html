About Me

Hi! My name is Andrew Krum. I am an aspiring software engineer with a desire to make a meaningful impact in people's lives using technology. I want to tell you about my journey and what drives me. I'll start with some of my interests and hobbies.

I love working out and staying healthy, and I even was a NASM certified personal trainer for a little while! My wife is a health and fitness coach, and we like to workout together in the morning around 5am. It's a great way to stay connected with eachother while doing something we both love.

When I was in highschool, I started learning how to solve a rubix cube. It became a hobby of mine and I still enjoy it today. A couple years ago I saw a friend of mine who had just gotten a 5x5 rubix cube and decided I wanted to try it out. I now have several rubix cubes but my favorite is the 5x5. Today, my best time for solving a 5x5 rubix cube is 8 minutes and 31 seconds, and my best time for solving a 3x3 rubix cube is 47 seconds.

I started getting into playing video games around the last couple years of highschool when my friend gave me his spare Xbox 360 along with a couple of games. My favorite was Halo 3, but I also enjoyed playing Call of Duty and a racing game called Project Gotham Racing. After I graduated from highschool, I started working for my grandpa and saved up enough money to buy an Xbox One. I played Forza Horizon, Destiny, Call of Duty, and Mortal Kombat. When Overwatch came out a few years later, I started playing it and eventually bought a PC to play it on. Although I enjoyed many other games too, I can say that Overwatch was by far my favorite.

Up until I bought my first PC, I really didn't have much interest in computer software or hardware. But because my older brother encouraged me to build my own gaming PC instead of buying one pre-built, I was able to experience the satisfaction of building my own computer from parts. I started seeing the depth of how complex each component was and it sparked my curiosity.

At that time, I was selling cars for a Volkswagen dealership. I was mostly concerned with getting better at communicating with people and learning the vehicles inside and out. I didn't really want to be a salesperson, but I enjoyed the challenge of helping people find the right vehicle for their needs. I got good at listening to people and understanding their needs, which helped me become a better salesperson.

I was still selling Volkswagens when COVID-19 hit. It was a tough time for everyone, and I had to adapt to the new circumstances. We had to change our approach to selling cars and focus on online communication. During this time, most of our vehicles were pre-sold before they arrived at the dealership.

I started to get tired of the repetitive nature of the car business and decided I would like to look at career opportunities in a different field. It didn't help that company I worked for had made several changes to the pay structure of my position. Many of the people I sold cars to worked in technology, and they inspired me to explore that field further. My dad is a retired software engineer who teaches IT classes now, and my older brother currently works in IT. Naturally, I had some interest in pursuing a career in technology. However, I wanted something I could be creative with.

My creative side became apparent to me when I was only a few years old and I started learning how to play the violin. I remember my teacher putting together a small tissue box with a ruler taped to it. She said I had to hold that under my chin for a certain amount of time each day so I would get used to holding the violin with my chin and not my hand. This was annoying, but it helped me develop the discipline and focus needed when it came time to learn on the actual instrument. I took lessons for a year or two but eventually grew more interested in piano instead. I took piano lessons for many years and got very good at it. I was blessed to have an incredibly gifted piano teacher and enjoyed playing piano for many years. I played piano solo for recitals or talent shows, for church services, for choirs, and even for an orchestra! I currently do not have a piano, but am saving up for one and am looking forward to when I can start playing it again.

Another creative outlet I have is painting. I did pencil drawings for a little while when I was a child, but when I met my wife, she had a side hustly selling paintings and that inspired me to take up the hobby. I had tried painting before but only for an art class in highschool and I didn't have materials outside of class to try it out in my free time. Painting has become a great way for me to express myself. I enjoy painting with acrylic paint, and my art style is mostly abstract. I like to use a realistic picture for reference, but paint it in my own abstract style focusing on colors and highlights and shadows.

Because of my creativite side, I felt that I could make a meaningful impact if I pursued a career that allowed me to be creative. After a lot of research and reflection, I decided to pursue a career in software engineering because I wanted to design and build complex things. At first, this seemed like a daunting task, but I was excited by the amount of different possibilities it presented. I talked to my dad and he recommended I start learning a programming language. Online, I saw many people talking about how much they use Python and decided to look more into it. My dad recommended a course that he found helpful on learning Python, I think it was on Udemy or some other online learning platform. The course was very basic, but because of my lack of knowledge, I was very slow at picking up the concepts. It became a struggle and I eventually stopped. Not for long though, because although I found the Python course monotonous, I really enjoyed the process of building something from scratch and being able to see it work in real time!

I looked online for more info on programming and the different programming languages commonly used, which led me to find a free web development course on freecodecamp.org. There were many other courses available besides web development, but because HTML and CSS are much more simple than learning Python, I began to really enjoy it! At that time I didn't understand the difference between a scripting language or an object-oriented language or a markup language but it was the fact that I could see what I was doing that made it so enjoyable.

At this point I was sure I didn't want to continue my career in sales. However, I didn't exactly know what to do next. It seemed like the more I learned about the world of technology, the more lost I became. There was what seemed like an expanding universe amount of knowledge that I didn't have and I began to feel overwhelmed. I wanted to switch careers, but I wasn't exactly sure where to put my focus. Since I hadn't been particularly interested in computers in the past, this was a new territory for me and I didn't have previous experience to know what I liked and didn't like. I knew the best way for me to learn what I needed to know in a structured manner would be to get a formal education in the field. But there was a problem; I had just bought a house, I had a car payment, my wife was starting a business, and I was struggling to make ends meet with my current job. I thought it was simply impossible to get the education I needed while working. I was at work between 50 and 60 hours a week.

I temporarily hung up the idea of pursuing a career in technology and decided to look for other higher-paying opportunities that I could succeed in with my current sales skills. In the back of my mind though, I knew this was delaying what felt like an inevitable sales burnout but at the time it seemed like my only option. I applied to several positions in the area and found a small company that was hiring and training mortgage loan officers. I had an interview and they agreed to hire me as long as I could pass the test for becoming a mortgage loan officer. He recommended an online resource for studying for the exam which I purchased and started studying and preparing for the exam. I didn't really care about the subject matter though, and my main reason for doing this was to obtain a better-paying job.

I wasn't really sure that this was the best path me, however. I kept on thinking about how much fun I had learning web development and how good it felt to put something together from scratch and be able to see it and use it over and over again! I also reached out to the realtor who helped me buy my house because I wanted to hear from his perspective what it would be like working in the mortgage business. He didn't have much to say in regards to the mortgage business but recommended reaching out to his friend who was a mortgage loan officer and currently was doing what I was studying to do. His friend told me that it's a very difficult field to start out in, and overall it would take a while to build a client base but that it was good pay once you can get it going. This was not so much a deterrent for me as it was eye opening for me. At that point I realized that pretty much whatever I decided to do next, I would need to be in it for the long haul. I couldn't expect it to be easy and I had to be okay putting in a lot of effort for a long amount of time. I knew the only way I would be able to do that was with something that I actually liked doing and found interesting.

I felt stuck. I really didn't want to stay where I was at, and because I was trying to find the perfect path forward but hadn't found it yet, I felt as though I couldn't leave either! One of my managers at the time recommended I read a book that he really liked about entrepreneurship. The book was called Unscripted by MJ DeMarco. Reading this book changed my perspective on money, on work, on my career, on just about everything I had learned as a child about financial success and taught me about the possibility of financial freedom. I started shifting my focus towards becoming what the book describes as a producer and not a consumer. It felt good to try and find ways that I could make life easier for other people and not just myself. I decided that no matter what it took I would go to school and get a formal education. I was going to do what I had planned on before, but with a new purpose. Not just to make my life better, but to make other people's lives better as well.

With my work schedule fluctuating so much, I knew that there was no way to go to school in person and so I decided to look at online colleges that had programs in Computer Science. I chose a Computer Science major instead of an IT or Software Engineering major due to the fact that there was so much I didn't know about the whole industry. Also, Computer Science has a lot more math than the other options I was considering. I wasn't scared of math, in fact I rather enjoy math.

After some research and pricing comparison, I applied for and got accepted to Western Governors University in Salt Lake City, Utah for their Bachelors program in Computer Science which is available 100% online. I've been studying online at WGU since 9/1/2025. I have had a great experience with WGU and of course am learning a lot. The fact that I am able to do everything online is both a blessing and a curse. A blessing because of the time flexibility and the ability to complete as many classes as I am able to within a given period of time. A curse because of the amount of willpower it takes to push through difficult classes without being in a classroom environment. However, I now see that as a blessing as well because it has made me more disciplined and taught me valuable lessons about how to stay focused even when it's hard to.

I am an aspiring software engineer. I count myself lucky to be starting my new career in the AI era, and I cannot wait to be a part of how the future of technology is changed with such an amazing tool.
